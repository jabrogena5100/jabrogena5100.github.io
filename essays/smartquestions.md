## Eric Raymond's View of Questions

In his article, [How to Ask Smart Questions](http://www.catb.org/esr/faqs/smart-questions.html), Eric Raymond goes in detail about the effects of asking questions, both smart and dumb, within hacker community forums. He explains that by asking smart questions, the askee receives more quality and quantity than an askee who asks non-smarter questions. For example, an askee who portrays object-deviation, in his/her question, is responded politely by a hacker (or hackers). 

He explains that users of the forum who asks smart questions were more likely to use more techincal terms, give more specifics, and proper letter cases. Compared to those who asked dumber questions, who would use upper case characters or 'grovel', these users were more likely to receive better answers from hackers. To see if this true or not, I searched through Stack Overflow to find a smart question as well as a dumb question and find out the effects. 

## Smart Question

Smart questions are easy to come by in Stack Overflow. In this online post [How to make app window same size at the MainForm](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/79879144/how-to-make-app-window-same-size-at-the-mainform), Raymond can feel satieted about this person. Firstly, the title looks formatted correctly, albeit not having many techincal terms. In his/her description (or concern), he/she gives a detailed overview of the problem that he's facing, which is changing the Windows display cale to 100%. He notes that he has "..tried: Changing MainForm.DesignTimePPI 120->96...." After OP sent it about an hour ago from my time, he received one reply and OP has given a reply back immediately. The OP should try out more solutions if he still has this much time to be replying to answers to his questions, in my opinion. 

## Non-Smart Question

In this Stack Overflow forum [arduino serial HELP ME](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/41289779/arduino-serial-help-me), there's somethings that tie this post in with Raymond's points. First, let's look at the title of the post, "arduino serial HELP ME". Raymond explicitly says to not say "help me"; nonetheless, use all-caps words. This post was posted nine years ago and by the title, someone like Raymond would be able to know what the post is about to look like. Sure enough, when you open the site then the person posts a code, expecting answers right away. OP doesn't give information about what solutions he tried thus far, so most likely he's going to end up with little-to-no response. 

## Conclusion

It's not rare to find non-smart questions in online forums the more you dive deeper into the past. Today, there's less posts like the one we mentioned earlier on because of Stack Overflow's optimized system that catches these types of questions. I think, as long as a post feels genuine that the OP wants to learn more, then there's not necessarily a 'dumb question'. There's many online resources out there instead of just community forums like Stack Overflow; including: AI assistants, youtube videos, better documentation, among others. Frankly, I think that now just posting even a question and hearing back takes a longer amount of time than finding the solution on your own.
